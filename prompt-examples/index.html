A clearer prompt gives AI a clearer job

A prompt is the request you give an AI tool. Use this short guide, then try five everyday examples and build a prompt for your own question. You can practice here before opening an AI service.

1. Give it a job

State your goal and, when useful, a role: “Be a patient tutor. Explain this to a beginner.”

2. Add useful background

Describe the audience and situation. Include relevant details, but leave out passwords, account numbers, and private records.

3. Set limits

Say what to avoid or be careful about: “Do not guess a price” or “Help me prepare questions, without telling me what to take.”

4. Choose a format

Ask for a table, checklist, short explanation, or step-by-step plan. Request plain language and examples.

5. Show a pattern

If style matters, provide two or three sample inputs and answers. This is called few-shot prompting. With no examples, it is zero-shot prompting.

6. Ask what to verify

Ask the tool to flag assumptions and identify claims to check. A detailed prompt can improve an answer, but cannot guarantee accuracy.

Keep longer chats on track

The context window is the limited amount of information an AI model can work with at once. It is measured in tokens, small chunks of text or data. Long chats can lose earlier details.

If an answer drifts, restate your goal, summarize the important facts, and repeat the format. Start a new chat when changing topics.

Comparing Health Information

Your goal: Understand the general differences between two options, prepare better questions for a doctor, and avoid treating an AI response as medical advice.

Compare with the basic prompt
I have heard that turmeric helps with joint pain, but my doctor recommended ibuprofen. Can you compare the pros and cons of both?

1. Make the prompt useful to you

Change the focus, then find the added preferences at the end of your completed prompt.

2. Your completed prompt

Your two choices update the preferences at the end. The goal, format, and boundaries stay included.

I am trying to prepare for a conversation with my doctor about joint pain. Please compare turmeric supplements and ibuprofen in plain language.

- Output format: a table with possible benefits, common risks, when to avoid or use caution, and questions to ask my doctor.

- Audience: an adult who is not a medical expert.

- Limits: do not give me a diagnosis or tell me what to take.

- Verification: if you mention studies or medical claims, tell me what kind of source I should verify, such as a doctor, pharmacist, or reputable medical site.

- Samples: include three example questions I could bring to my appointment.

Additional practice preferences:
- Questions for a doctor appointment
- Common risks and cautions

Compare with the basic prompt
Plan a 7-day trip to Paris for someone with limited mobility. Include affordable hotels near attractions, accessible transportation, and weather in October.

1. Make the prompt useful to you

Try a different pace or accessibility priority. Notice how the prompt gives the planner clearer limits.

2. Your completed prompt

Your two choices update the preferences at the end. The goal, format, and boundaries stay included.

Help me draft a 7-day Paris travel plan for an older adult with limited mobility. I want a comfortable pace, short travel distances, and accessible options when possible.

- Output format: a day-by-day table with morning, afternoon, evening, transportation notes, and rest breaks.

- Constraints: avoid long walking days, include indoor backup options, and keep the plan moderate rather than packed.

- Budget: suggest what to compare when looking for affordable hotels, but do not invent exact prices.

- Verification: list what I should confirm on official hotel, transit, museum, and weather sites before booking.

- Samples: include two sample hotel-location search phrases and three accessibility questions to ask before reserving.

Additional practice preferences:
- One main attraction with regular rest breaks
- Step-free entrances and elevator availability

Comparing Financial Options

Your goal: Understand basic tradeoffs between options and prepare questions for a financial professional without receiving personalized investment advice.

Compare with the basic prompt
Should I invest in bonds or CDs for steady income? I am risk-averse and retired.

1. Make the prompt useful to you

Choose a learning priority. Keep the boundary that asks for education, not a personal investment recommendation.

2. Your completed prompt

Your two choices update the preferences at the end. The goal, format, and boundaries stay included.

Explain the general differences between CDs and bonds for someone who wants steady income and prefers lower risk. Do not recommend a specific investment for me.

- Output format: a comparison table with safety, income, access to money, interest-rate risk, fees, insurance or guarantees, and questions to ask an advisor.

- Audience: a retired beginner who wants plain language.

- Constraints: avoid jargon unless you define it.

- Verification: tell me which details change over time, such as current rates, fees, tax rules, and account insurance limits.

- Samples: include two sample questions for a bank and two sample questions for a financial advisor.

Additional practice preferences:
- Access to money before the investment ends
- A bank representative and a financial advisor

Troubleshooting a Hobby Problem

Your goal: Get a step-by-step troubleshooting plan that starts with observation before jumping to a single answer.

Compare with the basic prompt
My tomato plants have yellow leaves. What is wrong, and how do I fix it without using chemicals?

1. Make the prompt useful to you

Use these sample observations or choose ones that fit your garden. Notice how observations add useful context.

2. Your completed prompt

Your two choices update the preferences at the end. The goal, format, and boundaries stay included.

Help me troubleshoot yellow leaves on tomato plants using a cautious, organic gardening approach.

- Output format: a checklist with possible causes, what to look for, simple tests, and low-risk next steps.

- Context: I want to avoid synthetic pesticides and avoid overcorrecting before I know the likely cause.

- Constraints: do not assume one cause from the symptom alone.

- Verification: tell me when I should consult a local university extension, nursery, or gardening expert.

- Samples: include three observations I can report back, such as watering pattern, leaf location, or visible pests.

Additional practice preferences:
- In outdoor containers
- Mostly lower leaves are yellow

Explain how Medicare Part B works, but pretend I am a beginner. Include examples of what it covers.

1. Make the prompt useful to you

Choose a learning focus and a fictional example. No Medicare number or personal records are needed.

2. Your completed prompt

Your two choices update the preferences at the end. The goal, format, and boundaries stay included.

Explain Medicare Part B in beginner-friendly language for someone who wants to understand the basics before reviewing official Medicare information.

- Output format: start with a one-paragraph overview, then provide a table with what it generally covers, what it may not cover, possible costs to ask about, and where to verify details.

- Audience: a beginner who may not know insurance vocabulary.

- Constraints: define key terms such as premium, deductible, coinsurance, and outpatient care.

- Verification: remind me which details must be checked with Medicare.gov or a qualified benefits counselor.

- Samples: include three example questions I could ask before making enrollment or coverage decisions.

Additional practice preferences:

Build Your Own Prompt

Start with any question you want to explore. Use the same six steps from Start here to give the AI a clearer job. Only your question is required; the other steps help you add detail.

Use a fictional situation if you prefer. Leave out passwords, account numbers, and private records. Your draft is not saved when you reload or close this page, so copy it somewhere safe if you want to keep it.

1. Give it a job

Describe your question or task. You can also suggest a role, such as a patient tutor.

2. Add useful background

Describe the situation and who the answer is for. Leave out names, account numbers, and private records.

3. Set limits

Consider time, cost, detail, or actions you want to avoid.

4. Choose a format

Every prompt asks for plain language, defined unfamiliar terms, and a practical example.

5. Show a pattern

Provide a sample answer or two or three input/output examples if a particular style matters. Otherwise, leave this blank.

6. Ask what to verify

Keep the suggested instructions or replace them with details specific to your question.
